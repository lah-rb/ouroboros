## DESCRIPTION

A single-panel line graph showing four X-ray energy spectra (photon counts versus photon energy) overlaid on the same axes — the kind of plot produced by an energy-dispersive X-ray detector. There are no panel letters, no subfigure divisions, no title text inside or above the plot, and no error bars.

The horizontal axis is labelled `Energy, KeV` (printed with a capital K and a capital V, i.e. `KeV`, not the conventional `keV`). It is LINEAR, running from 0 at the left frame to 10 at the right frame, with numeric tick labels at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Heavier vertical gridlines fall on each labelled even value and lighter vertical gridlines subdivide these at every 0.5 keV.

The vertical axis is labelled `# of Counts`, printed rotated 90° counter-clockwise along the left side (reading bottom-to-top). This axis is LOGARITHMIC: only two tick values are printed, `1000` (upper) and `100` (lower), and the horizontal gridlines between them form the characteristic log-decade pattern (nine minor lines per decade, bunching toward the top of each decade). The decade spacing is uniform, so the top frame of the plot sits at 10 000 counts and the bottom frame at roughly 20 counts; neither endpoint is printed.

Grid and frame are drawn in blue/violet on a white background. A legend sits in the upper-left corner, inside a rectangle with a thin dark outline that overlaps and occludes the top-left of the plot area. It carries four colour-swatch-plus-text entries, all beginning with the word `Amphibole` followed by two angle values, the first positive and the second negative, each with a degree symbol.

The four spectra are near-coincident in shape and differ mainly in amplitude. From left to right the composition is: all traces plunging steeply off the top of the frame at the extreme left (below about 0.3 keV, where the light-blue trace shows a near-vertical segment at about 0.2 keV running the full height of the plot); a broad low-count region between about 1 and 2.5 keV containing a small feature near 1.6–1.7 keV; a medium peak near 2.9 keV; a large sharp peak near 3.6 keV; a small shoulder near 4.0 keV and a medium peak near 4.45 keV; a small peak near 4.95 keV; a deep valley around 5.2–5.5 keV; the tallest peak of the figure near 6.4 keV immediately followed by a second large peak near 7.0 keV, the two separated only by a partial valley near 6.6–6.7 keV; a small peak near 7.65 keV; and beyond about 8.2 keV all four traces collapse into a dense, spiky noise band of a few tens of counts that continues to the right frame. The traces are visibly noisy (channel-to-channel spikes) rather than smooth, especially in the low-count regions.
